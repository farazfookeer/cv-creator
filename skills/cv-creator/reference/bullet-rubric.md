# Bullet rubric

Used in **bullets mode** and during **create mode** when filling experience sections.

## The rule

Every bullet should be **action verb + quantified result + impact**.

- **Action verb** — strong, specific, past tense for past roles. ("Led," "Reduced," "Designed," "Negotiated," "Diagnosed," "Closed," "Authored.") Avoid weak openers: "Helped," "Worked on," "Was responsible for," "Assisted with."
- **Quantified result** — a number. Money, percentage, count, time, scale. If you don't have one, ask the user for one before writing the bullet.
- **Impact** — what changed because of this. Customer outcome, business outcome, team outcome. The "so what."

A bullet missing one of the three legs is incomplete. A bullet missing two is filler.

## Anatomy

```
[ACTION VERB] [object — what you did to/with what] [QUANTIFIED RESULT — by how much] [IMPACT — why it mattered]

Example:
Reduced p99 checkout latency from 480ms to 110ms by replacing N+1 ORM calls with a batched DataLoader,
[VERB]   [object — latency]                       [HOW]
unblocking a 3% conversion lift.
[IMPACT]
```

## Before / after examples (general)

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| Worked on the deployment pipeline. | Cut deploy time from 22 to 4 minutes by parallelizing the test stage; saved the team ~30 engineer-hours/week. |
| Helped train new staff. | Authored 4-week onboarding curriculum and ran weekly office hours for 12 new hires; first-PR-merged time fell from 11 to 4 days. |
| Was responsible for customer accounts. | Managed $4.8M ARR portfolio of 12 enterprise accounts; drove 128% NRR and zero logo churn over 24 months. |
| Did process improvement work. | Redesigned the claims-intake workflow (Lean + Six Sigma); cut median cycle time from 9 to 3 days across 4 regional offices. |
| Worked with stakeholders. | Aligned 6 product, eng, and design leads on a 3-quarter platform roadmap; secured $2.4M of new funding from the executive review. |

## Verb bank (use; don't recycle)

**Strategy / leadership** — Led, Directed, Spearheaded *(use sparingly)*, Founded, Built, Scaled, Grew, Launched, Architected, Established, Pioneered

**Quantitative impact** — Increased, Reduced, Cut, Doubled, Tripled, Halved, Drove, Generated, Saved, Earned, Recovered

**Improvement / change** — Refactored, Redesigned, Streamlined, Modernized, Migrated, Consolidated, Eliminated, Automated, Standardized

**Communication / collaboration** — Negotiated, Influenced, Aligned, Persuaded, Authored, Presented, Briefed, Advised

**Technical / craft** — Designed, Developed, Implemented, Deployed, Optimized, Diagnosed, Debugged, Instrumented, Hardened

**Sales / commercial** — Closed, Sold, Onboarded, Renewed, Expanded, Sourced, Pitched, Negotiated

**Clinical / care** — Diagnosed, Triaged, Stabilized, Administered, Coordinated, Educated, Documented, Escalated

## Patterns by industry

- **Tech:** lead with engineering verb → end with system or business outcome (latency, uptime, conversion, cost).
- **Sales:** lead with attainment % → break into deal counts and ACV → name a stack-rank or signature win.
- **Nursing / clinical:** lead with patient population/ratio → name procedure/equipment → close with outcome (CAUTI rate, mortality, patient satisfaction).
- **Finance:** lead with deal/portfolio scope → name model/method → close with $ outcome.
- **Trades:** lead with site/scope → specific technical work performed → close with safety/inspection result.
- **Creative:** lead with project type/client → describe what you made → close with reach, conversion, award, or press.
- **Executive:** lead with org-level outcome → name transformation → close with $/% scope and timeline.

## Anti-patterns to watch

- **"Responsible for X."** Empty. Strip and rewrite as what you *did* with that responsibility.
- **"Excellent communication skills."** Don't tell — show via the work.
- **"Team player."** Same.
- **Stacked adjectives.** "Innovative, dynamic, results-driven leader." Cut all of them; the bullets should make the case.
- **Acronym soup.** Spell out on first use unless the audience is guaranteed to know.
- **Fake precision.** "Increased efficiency by 47.3%." If you can't justify the precision, round to a believable figure or rephrase.
- **Borrowed credit.** "Drove $50M revenue" when you were one of 30 contributors reads as inflation. Be honest about your role: "Contributed to," "Co-led," "Supported."

## When the user has no number

Don't fabricate. Ask:
- "How many people / accounts / patients / dollars / requests?"
- "What was the before-state? After-state?"
- "Who noticed? What changed for them?"
- "How long did it take, and how long *would* it have taken?"

If after honest digging the bullet has no quantifiable hook, drop the bullet — it's not pulling weight. A leaner CV with strong bullets beats a longer one padded with weak ones.
