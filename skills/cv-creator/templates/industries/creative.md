# Creative overlay (designer / writer / video / brand)

Apply on top of any region template. Covers graphic/UX/UI/product designers, copywriters, content creators, video editors, art directors, brand strategists.

## Section emphasis

- **Portfolio link** in the header is the most important thing on the entire CV. Without it, you're not getting an interview.
- **Selected Work** section listing 4–8 named projects (client, role, scope, outcome). Often more important than the experience section itself.
- **Tools** — current creative stack (Figma, Adobe CC, Webflow, Premiere, Cinema 4D, etc.). Tier them: expert / proficient / familiar.
- **Awards / publications** if relevant (D&AD, Cannes, ADC, Awwwards, Communication Arts, FastCo Innovation, Type Directors Club).
- **Visual quality of the CV itself** — recruiters in design fields judge typography, hierarchy, and whitespace on the document. The CV is a portfolio piece. (Caveat: keep an ATS-safe Markdown/text version for parser screens.)

## Section order (override)

1. **Header** — name, title, location, **portfolio URL**, contact
2. **Profile** — voice/POV, what you make, the kind of problems you solve (3–5 lines)
3. **Selected Work** — 4–8 highlighted projects
4. **Experience** — agency / in-house roles
5. **Tools** — software stack, tiered
6. **Awards & Press**
7. **Education**
8. **Speaking / Teaching** *(if applicable)*

## Creative-specific gotchas

- **Portfolio URL > the CV itself.** If the link is broken on submission day, you're invisible. Test it.
- **Two-version strategy:** ship a designed PDF for direct-to-hiring-manager applications, **and** a Markdown / plain-text version for ATS-screened roles. Most large companies' applicant portals will mangle a designed PDF.
- **Don't list "good eye" or "passionate about design."** Show, don't tell — the work in the portfolio is the proof.
- **Anonymize NDA work** ("Series-B fintech," "Fortune-50 retailer") rather than naming a brand you can't disclose. Recruiters understand.
- **For UX/Product designers:** lean closer to tech overlay — quantified outcomes (conversion, retention, task-success rates), not just visuals. Hiring managers at product companies want evidence of business impact, not aesthetic preference.
- **Keep text versions ATS-clean** even if the visual version is a design statement: avoid columns, embedded text-as-image, and headers/footers in the parseable copy.
