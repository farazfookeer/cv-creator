# Academic CV skeleton

**Length:** 4–10+ pages. Comprehensive, not condensed. Junior PhD candidate: 3–4. Tenure-track applicant: 6–10. Full professor: can run 20+.
**Photo:** No (US/UK academic norm). Some EU/Asian institutions tolerate it; default no.
**Personal details:** Name, institutional affiliation, office address, email, ORCID, Google Scholar, personal website.
**Date format:** YYYY (year-only is standard for academic CVs; granular months are noise here).
**Bullets:** Usually NO bullets in publications/talks — those are formatted entries. Bullets only in "Service" or "Teaching" if you need to elaborate.

## Section order (default — vary by field)

1. **Header** — name, affiliation, contact, ORCID, Scholar, website
2. **Education** — PhD first; advisor name; dissertation title
3. **Academic Appointments** — postdocs, faculty positions, visiting roles
4. **Research Interests** *(2–4 lines)*
5. **Publications** — most important section, often subdivided:
   - Peer-reviewed journal articles
   - Conference proceedings (if discipline values them — CS/engineering yes; humanities no)
   - Book chapters
   - Books / monographs
   - Preprints / under review
   - Reverse chronological within each subsection; bold or underline your name
6. **Grants & Funding** — title, agency, role, amount, dates
7. **Awards & Honors**
8. **Invited Talks** — separate from contributed talks
9. **Conference Presentations**
10. **Teaching** — courses taught (institution, term, role: instructor/TA, enrollment)
11. **Mentoring / Advising** — PhD/MS/undergrad supervised
12. **Service** — to discipline (peer review, committees), to institution, to community
13. **Professional Memberships**
14. **Languages** *(if relevant — humanities, area studies)*
15. **References** — list 3–5 with full contact info (academic CVs DO list referees, unlike industry)

## Discipline-specific notes

- **CS / Engineering:** Conference papers count as much as or more than journal articles. List them as a top-tier section, not buried.
- **Humanities:** Books and book chapters dominate. List monographs first, then edited volumes, then articles.
- **Life sciences / medicine:** Often add **Clinical Experience** between Appointments and Research Interests. Authorship position matters — first/last/corresponding should be flagged.
- **Math / theoretical physics:** Author lists are alphabetical; first-authorship convention doesn't apply. Don't try to bold yourself "first."
- **Field-specific quirks:** Some fields (econ, finance) want a separate "Working Papers" section; some (law) want "Bar Admissions"; some (creative arts) want "Exhibitions/Performances." Ask the user.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Industry-style bullets in publications.** Don't write "Authored a high-impact paper that..." — just list the citation.
- **Compressing to 2 pages.** Industry instinct; wrong here. Hiring committees expect comprehensiveness.
- **Hiding low-prestige venues.** List everything peer-reviewed; don't curate.
- **Omitting failed grants.** Listing submitted-but-unfunded is sometimes appropriate (shows you're applying); discipline-dependent.
