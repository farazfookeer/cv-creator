# UK / EU CV skeleton

**Length:** 2 pages standard. 1 page acceptable for entry-level; 3+ only for senior academic-adjacent roles.
**Photo:** UK — no. Continental EU (DE/FR/ES/IT) — optional, increasingly skipped at international companies; default to **no** unless target country strongly expects one (Germany → see germany.md).
**Personal details:** Name, city/country, phone (+country code), email, LinkedIn. DOB optional and trending toward omission. Nationality/work-authorization useful if applying cross-border.
**Date format:** Month YYYY or MM/YYYY. ISO (YYYY-MM) acceptable.
**Bullets:** 3–6 per role; UK culture tolerates slightly less aggressive self-promotion than US — still quantify, but verb choice can be more measured ("led" vs "spearheaded").

## Section order (default)

1. **Header**
2. **Personal Profile / Summary** — 3–5 lines, more common in UK than US
3. **Key Skills** *(optional, often placed early in UK)*
4. **Professional Experience**
5. **Education** *(higher emphasis in UK — institution prestige matters more)*
6. **Certifications / Professional Memberships**
7. **Languages** *(important within EU)*
8. **Interests** *(optional; very UK-traditional, skip if space-tight)*
9. **References** *("Available on request" — do not list referees)*

## Region-specific gotchas

- **Class of degree (UK):** "2:1" or "First" is expected; "GPA 3.7" reads as foreign. Convert.
- **Dates:** Continental Europe defaults to DD/MM/YYYY; spell out the month to avoid ambiguity for US readers screening EU candidates.
- **References:** Do not list. "Available on request" is the standard.
- **UK humility:** Bullets should still quantify, but choose verbs slightly less aggressive than US-style ("delivered" over "spearheaded"; "improved" over "transformed").
- **Europass:** A formal EU-standard format exists. It's rarely required for private sector and looks generic — only use if the employer specifically asks for it (typical for EU institutions, some public-sector roles).
